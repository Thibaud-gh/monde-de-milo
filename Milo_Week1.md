**Chapter:** Bonjour, Milo ! / Le panier de Milo  
**Learner:** Age six, absolute French beginner  
**Format:** Three parent-led sessions of approximately 30 minutes, optional 5–10-minute iPad practice, and one short illustrated French audio story.

## 1. Learning objectives

By the end of Week 1, aim for the child to:

- Recognise **une pomme**, **une banane**, **de l'eau**, and **du pain** when heard.
- Understand **« Montre-moi… »** (show me) versus **« Donne-moi… »** (give me), initially with demonstrations and increasingly without them.
- Respond to simple requests using real objects and the iPad controls.
- Recognise **« Bonjour ! »**, **« Au revoir ! »** and **« Bravo ! »**.
- Optionally attempt some words aloud, without pressure.

This is a playful target, not a pass/fail standard. Comprehension comes before speech.

## 2. Core language

| French | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Une pomme | An apple | Lesson 1 |
| Une banane | A banana | Lesson 1 |
| De l'eau | Water | Lesson 1 |
| Du pain | Bread | Introduce Lesson 2 |
| Montre-moi la pomme ! | Show me the apple! | Point/show in real life; touch-and-hold in game |
| Donne-moi la banane ! | Give me the banana! | Hand over in real life; drag to Milo in game |
| Où est la pomme ? | Where is the apple? | Optional variation |
| Bonjour ! / Au revoir ! | Hello! / Goodbye! | Opening/closing ritual |
| Bravo ! | Well done! | Encouragement |

Use grammatically natural phrases with the other foods: *Montre-moi la banane / le pain / l'eau* and *Donne-moi la pomme / le pain / l'eau*. Don't teach grammar rules yet.

## 3. Lesson 1 — Bonjour, Milo ! (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Recognise apple, banana and water; introduce *montre-moi* with gestures.

**Materials:** Teddy bear (Milo), apple, banana, glass/bottle of water, tray.

| Time | Activity | Parent guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Meet Milo | Wave: « Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Milo ! » Briefly explain in English that Milo speaks French. |
| 5–12 min | Discover objects | Present each item with « Une pomme ! », « Une banane ! », « De l'eau ! ». Use gestures and funny voices; don't require repetition. |
| 12–20 min | Show me | Demonstrate « Montre-moi la pomme ! » by pointing; ask her to point/show. Repeat with the same three items, gradually reducing hints. |
| 20–26 min | Silly Papa | Hold a banana and ask « Une pomme ? »; let her correct you in any way. |
| 26–30 min | Victory | Ask her to identify the objects, praise effort, say « Au revoir, Milo ! ». |

**Speaking opportunity:** Model the food words and invite, but never demand, repetition.

**Game after session:** Activity 1, *Trouve !* — see section 6.

## 4. Lesson 2 — Le panier de Milo (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Add bread and introduce *donne-moi*, explicitly contrasting showing and giving.

**Materials:** Milo, apple, banana, water, bread, basket.

| Time | Activity | Parent guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Review | « Montre-moi la pomme ! » and other known objects. |
| 5–12 min | New food | Introduce « Du pain ! »; mix it with the first three items. |
| 12–20 min | Give to Milo | Model « Donne-moi la pomme ! » by handing it to Milo. Repeat with other items. |
| 20–26 min | Same object, different action | Alternate « Montre-moi la banane ! » (point/show) and « Donne-moi la banane ! » (hand over). Restore the object between turns. Use visual demonstrations, then fade them. |
| 26–30 min | Review | Mix a few instructions, praise and say goodbye. |

**Important:** If she gives an item when asked to show it, demonstrate the difference gently. Distinguish misunderstanding the verb from not knowing the noun.

**Game after session:** Activity 2, *Donne à Milo !*, followed by a few mixed-action trials. **Introduce the Week 1 audio story together**, then let her replay it freely.

## 5. Lesson 3 — La mission de Milo (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Consolidate vocabulary and distinguish *montre-moi* from *donne-moi* without relying on predictable patterns.

**Materials:** Milo, four food items, basket, hiding places around the room.

| Time | Activity | Parent guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Warm-up | Greet Milo; quick recognition review. |
| 5–12 min | Treasure hunt | Hide foods; ask « Où est la banane ? » and « Montre-moi la pomme ! ». |
| 12–22 min | Milo's mission | Milo needs ingredients. Mix « Montre-moi… » and « Donne-moi… » with the **same** objects. Ask one instruction at a time. |
| 22–27 min | Role reversal | Let her give you simple instructions or name the food; accept single words and model full phrases. |
| 27–30 min | Celebrate | Short review, « Bravo ! », « Au revoir ! ». |

**Speaking opportunity:** Encourage voluntary naming and simple requests; no formal pronunciation scoring.

**Game after session:** Activity 3, *La mission de Milo*.

## 6. Week 1 iPad game — Le panier de Milo

### Shared game rules

- Child-facing interface entirely in French, with very little text; clear spoken prompts and a replay-audio button.
- No child-visible numerical scores, perfection badges, accuracy percentages, or mistake counters. Praise curiosity and completion; mistakes are safe and retryable.
- Put the next adventure prominently on the home screen, but keep all earlier games and stories freely replayable. Start unfamiliar activities with easy, familiar interactions and guidance.
- Large pictures of food, Milo and his basket; no reading required.
- **Show:** « Montre-moi la pomme ! » → **touch and hold** the correct object briefly; it glows but stays put.
- **Give:** « Donne-moi la pomme ! » → **drag** the correct object to Milo; Milo accepts it and says « Merci ! ».
- Demonstrate each gesture visually before testing understanding. Reduce hints over time.
- Reuse the **same food item** for both actions; mix actions within a single activity once the controls are learned.
- A stray tap or incomplete drag is not automatically a language error. Provide a gentle second chance or hint.
- **Touch-and-hold is a provisional interaction hypothesis:** observe the child's response and revise if unnatural.

### Activity 1 — Trouve ! (after Lesson 1)

Three food illustrations are visible. Milo says a word or « Montre-moi… ». The child touches and holds the named object. Early turns show a hand animation; later turns do not. Reward comprehension with a glow and cheerful audio. Focus on noun recognition and learning the *show* gesture.

### Activity 2 — Donne à Milo ! (after Lesson 2)

Four food objects are visible. Milo asks « Donne-moi… ». The child drags the item to Milo. Once this is familiar, introduce mixed *montre-moi* and *donne-moi* requests with identical objects. Do not reveal the action through separate screen layouts.

### Activity 3 — La mission de Milo (after Lesson 3)

A short illustrated café mission. Milo gives a sequence of **single** spoken instructions, alternating *show* and *give* with familiar food. The child performs each before proceeding. Add short sequences of two instructions only as an optional challenge. Finish with a small celebration. All three activities remain replayable.

### Sample trials

| Spoken instruction | Correct object | Correct action |
|---|---|---|
| Montre-moi la pomme ! | Apple | Touch and hold |
| Donne-moi la pomme ! | Apple | Drag to Milo |
| Donne-moi la banane ! | Banana | Drag to Milo |
| Montre-moi la banane ! | Banana | Touch and hold |
| Montre-moi le pain ! | Bread | Touch and hold |
| Donne-moi l'eau ! | Water | Drag to Milo |

## 7. Week 1 illustrated story — Milo cherche sa pomme

**Purpose:** A relaxed, non-assessed French listening experience reinforcing food vocabulary and greetings. **No quizzes, questions requiring answers, scores or pass/fail gates.** Introduce after Lesson 2; allow replay any time. Target 5–8 static illustrated pages, roughly 1–2 minutes of natural, expressive French narration. A simple MVP may use six pages and shorter audio.

**Storyboard (initial draft; revise phrasing for natural audio and illustrations):**

| Page | French narration / on-screen caption | Illustration direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | « Bonjour, Éva ! Je cherche ma pomme. » | Milo thinks of his missing apple in the café. |
| 2 | « Sur la table ? Non, c’est de l’eau ! » | Milo checks the water on the table. |
| 3 | « Sur l’étagère ? Non, c’est une banane ! » | Milo checks the banana on the shelf. |
| 4 | « Et dans le panier ? Voyons… » | The basket cloth lifts; the apple is still hidden. |
| 5 | « Ah ! Ma pomme ! Merci, Éva ! » | The apple is revealed inside the basket. |
| 6 | « Au revoir, Éva ! À bientôt ! » | Milo waves with the apple in his hand. |

The narrative is intentionally very simple for a complete beginner. A small amount of context-supported language (*je m'appelle*, *et*, *ah*) is acceptable; do not require her to memorise it. Keep illustrations semantically precise (especially the apple's location on pages 4–5). Future stories should reuse prior vocabulary while gently adding new material.

**Story player:** Full-width static illustration, short French text below or overlaid legibly, automatic optional narration, replay current page audio, previous/next page, optional autoplay, and repeat story. Child can turn pages without reading. French-only child-facing labels, preferably icon-based controls. No quiz at the end.

**Analytics:** Record story opened, pages visited, completed and replayed, optionally duration; **never interpret these events as comprehension or speaking ability**. The parent can optionally discuss or act out the story during Lesson 3.

**Progression approach:** Feature the new story as Milo's next adventure while preserving easy access to familiar stories. Never lock an old story or force a new one; parents can co-read new material. New adventures should begin with familiar elements. No child-facing perfect-score incentives anywhere in the app.

## 8. Progress tracking and parent notes

**Automatically capture:** activity, phrase, object, requested action, selected object, performed action, hint use, outcome and repeat attempts. Also capture story page views, completions and replays as engagement-only events; distinguish new versus familiar content use. Report **object correctness**, **action correctness**, and **both correct** separately. Distinguish assisted and unassisted attempts.

**After each parent session:** For each target word or expression, optionally record **says independently**, **says with help**, or **not yet**; note whether she understood the spoken request and any control confusion observed in the game. Do not infer speaking ability from iPad accuracy.

## 9. Reinforcement at home

Use a minute or two during actual snacks or meals:

- « Montre-moi la pomme ! » — she points or shows.
- « Donne-moi la banane ! » — she hands it over.
- « Où est le pain ? » — she finds it.
- « Bonjour ! » / « Au revoir ! » — greet Milo or each other.

Sometimes use the same food with different actions. Keep it light, and clarify in English when necessary.

## 10. End-of-week review and next steps

Check playfully whether she recognises the four foods and can distinguish *show* versus *give* when asked about the same object. Note whether she attempts any French aloud, and which activities she enjoys. Review the dashboard for recurring noun-versus-action errors, while considering gesture/control confusion.

If comprehension is developing, repeat Week 1 language before introducing much new material. If ready, Week 2 introduces *« J'ai faim ! »*, *« J'ai soif ! »* and simple *oui/non* responses while retaining the Week 1 vocabulary and actions.

- [ ] Same objects can be used for either requested action without layout clues.
- [ ] Action and object mistakes are logged separately.
- [ ] Tutorial hints can be shown initially and faded.
- [ ] Accidental taps/incomplete drags receive forgiving feedback.
- [ ] Three Week 1 activities are playable and replayable.
- [ ] Week 1 illustrated story is playable with six static pages, French text and narration, page navigation and audio replay; no quiz.
- [ ] Story progress and replays are tracked separately from language comprehension.
- [ ] Child-facing UI has no accuracy percentages, perfect-score rewards or mistake counters.
- [ ] Home screen gently highlights the next adventure without restricting familiar content.
- [ ] New activities begin with guided, easy interactions; perfect accuracy is not required to progress.
