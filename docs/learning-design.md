# Lessons that need the intended understanding

Before adding a game, ask: could a child get every answer right while ignoring the new language? If so, change the task, not just its dialogue.

- Introduce new nouns with familiar instructions. Teach new sentence contrasts with familiar nouns, then combine them.
- Counterbalance the contrast: both foods appear as what Milo has and wants; the wanted clause appears first and last. Shuffle picture positions. Remove answer-revealing pictures during independent turns.
- Guided teaching, supported retries, and independent responses are separate observations. Choosing the wrong role in a sentence is not automatically evidence of an unknown food word or gesture.
- Understanding games require only a physical response. Speaking games are separate, parent-observed role-play. No automatic speech judgement; a word or gesture is welcome without being logged as a whole sentence.
- Practise both “J’ai…” and “Je voudrais…” separately before expecting them together. Keep reviewing the phrases across sessions even as vocabulary expands.
- Each game has two introductions, then a replayable third stage, with one star per stage. Stars show participation through a stage, not language mastery.

## Stories need their own reason to exist

A story needs a small problem, anticipation, a playful surprise and a resolution. It should not be the game repeated without interaction. Target phrases belong naturally inside the plot; avoid a quiz at every page.

## Revised Session 2

Two new foods: milk and cheese, alongside apple, banana, bread and water.

Understanding has three stages. In stage 1, the child taps what Milo has under the cloth and drags him the familiar food he requests. Either choice can come first and be changed until both are made. The game then reveals what Milo has and marks both choices. Stage 2 mixes “Je voudrais”, “Donne-moi” and “Montre-moi” with apple, banana, milk and cheese. Stage 3 returns to the two-choice interaction with all six foods and both give request forms. Speaking: describe a chosen possession, then request something different, using a whole model, a starter, and eventually no supplied model.

The revised listening progression uses v3 keys; the speaking progression keeps its v2 keys. Previous attempts remain in the journal, but finishing the former noun-matching lesson does not bypass the new sentence lesson. Session 1 keeps its original progress and four foods. The separate parent lesson remains screen-free.
